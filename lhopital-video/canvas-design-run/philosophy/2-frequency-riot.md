# Frequency Riot（频率暴动）

A philosophy of controlled detonation — the visual language of the bass drop, of
sound made violent and joyful at once. It takes the street's raw energy and
disciplines it with the precision of a master typographer, proving that chaos,
when meticulously composed, becomes rhythm.

**Space and form.** Diagonal force cuts the field. Bands of pure energy slice
across the composition at speed, fracturing the grid into before and after, like
a waveform clipped at maximum amplitude. Form is tension: heavy mass shoved
against open black, every angle calculated, nothing accidental despite the
apparent violence.

**Color and material.** Acid against black. A near-fluorescent chartreuse and a
hot magenta collide on a matte black ground — two frequencies beating against
each other, the optical buzz of complementary clash. Color is loud but never
muddy; each hue is calibrated with painstaking attention so the vibration reads
as intentional, not noise.

**Scale and rhythm.** Typography as amplitude — a single word blown to monumental,
condensed, almost overflowing its margins, paired with a ticking register of
small equalizer marks and tight clinical labels. The huge and the tiny, the
shout and the whisper, set in master-level counterpoint.

**Hierarchy.** Mass leads, color punctuates, fine print grounds. Text is reduced
to essential gestures integrated into the architecture — a name, a date, a place.
The result must look like the work of someone at the absolute top of their field:
aggressive yet flawless, every edge crisp, nothing overlapping, a riot held in
perfect compositional discipline.
